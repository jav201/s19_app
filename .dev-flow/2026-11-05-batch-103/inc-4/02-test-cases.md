# Inc 4 — test cases (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3), E1

One case per coverage item `<requirement id>/<n>` of `01-requirements.md`, plus the black-box cases `AT-B103-I4-1` and `AT-B103-I4-2`. Written at E1, before any test: **Tests** is empty, **Coverage** is `gap` and **Result** is `not_run` everywhere (E4 fills them). Proposed test files (`E1-slice.md` §4): G-T1 `tests/test_b103_label_match.py` (every case but those of G-T2), G-T2 `tests/test_b103_label_match_timing.py` (R-A2L-MATCH.35/1, .36/1, .40/1, .40/2 and .40/3 with `serial_timing`; .37/1, a byte bound, without it; .40/3 added by the E5 correction, E5S-01, with Tests empty, Coverage `gap` and Result `not_run` until E4). Class: *robustness* for the cases of an `If` row and for the edge inputs the brief names (non-ASCII, brackets and pattern characters, a query longer than every name); *normal* otherwise.

Every expected result was derived from the rows by hand and then compared with the ranked lists of the E1 oracle (a throwaway reference of the rows under the recommended rule order, D-3 B): `evidence-e1/i4e1-pilot3-transcript.txt` §1–§2. The oracle and the prepared-index variant agree on 409 generated queries at 4 limits (§3: 0 mismatches). An E4 test asserts the values below, never the oracle.

**Notation.** A ranked list is written `[entry; entry; …]`; an entry is `NAME · rule · spans`, and a `fuzzy` entry adds `· e<k>` (its total edits). Spans are `(start,end)` offsets into the name. `[]` is the empty ranked list. "Exactly" means the whole list, in that order, nothing more.
