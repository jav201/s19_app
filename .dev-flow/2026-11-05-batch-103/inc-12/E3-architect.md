## 3. Answers to the questions routed to E3

**UX — column order Address · Length · Tag.** Acceptable, and the only order that meets the ruling.
- **Tag first and fixed does not work.** The fixed area (Tag *w* + 4, plus Address, plus Length) would take the whole visible width, so a horizontal scroll could reveal nothing.
- **What makes the name easy to find.** It starts at a constant x for the session: 21 on 32-bit pages (Address 12 + Length 8 + its padding cell), 26 with a 64-bit address. Three cues mark it: its in-image glyph `✓`/`·`, the bright `VALUE` style against the cyan Address, and its label.
- **The cost.** The name leaves the eye's start at x 0, and at 80 columns it gets 12 to 14 of 36 to 38 cells. The operator judges this with E3A-04's capture.

**UX — is the middle cut readable?**
- ***w* ≥ 20 (95 columns and up, with or without a scrollbar):** yes. Both ends carry words (`SYN_MAP_LL…LLLLLLL_END`).
- ***w* 12 to 14:** the head is only the namespace (`ASAM.`) and the tail only the last word (`…NTICAL`). That is readable but does not identify the tag (E3A-04).
- **Glyphs:** `…` is on the measured Windows Terminal 1-cell list (tui-design DENSITY), so the cut keeps the grid.

**E2-7 — a floor at *w* ≤ 9 (drop other columns instead)?** No floor in Inc 12.
- **Dropping other columns gains nothing.** The columns after Tag are already beyond the visible width whenever *w* < 34, so they do not compete for it.
- **The only real competitors are ruled out.** They are Address and Length (whole by the ruling) and the pane split (R-TUI-037.1, Inc 7's layout).
- **Any other floor reopens the defect.** Keeping the Tag cell at a fixed minimum would push it past the visible edge, the unmarked cut R-TUI-042a.5 removes.
- **The real threshold is *w* ≈ 12, not 9.** That is the ordinary 80-column page with a scrollbar (E3A-04).
- **Where the identity lives instead:** the cursor row, the detail card, filter, find and the copy.
- **What to do:** show it to the operator with numbers at AT-B103-I12-2. If they object, route a narrow layout to Inc 7.

**E2-4 — widths only grow during a session: acceptable or a defect?** Acceptable: record it as (a), not a defect.
- **The rows hold.** Every row computes *w* from the actual widths, and R-TUI-042a.4 is safer this way: a column never becomes narrower than any address it has shown.
- **The fit depends on it.** Its termination rests on monotone widths (E3A-09).
- **Option (b) costs more.** It writes the `Column.content_width` dataclass field (not a documented setter) and reopens that termination question.
- **The cost of (a):**
  - After a page or file with a 64-bit address, later 32-bit pages lose 4 name cells for the session.
  - The Tag column keeps its widest width, 38 cells, after a shrink: scrolled right at 80 columns, Source starts after up to 22 blank cells (`[O deep]`: Tag column 38 for 16-cell Tag cells). The in-place rewrite of E3A-06 removes the Tag column part (38 → 16, `[O inplace]`); Address and Length stay grow-only.
- **Record it** in E1's "Not decisions" and the backlog.

**E2-5 — the two case texts.**
- **Accept (a)**, worded "joined without their line ends, the card's painted lines hold the 48-character name, and one painted line holds `0x1C2D3E00` whole". AT-B103-I12-1's "every `0x` run equals a declared address" already catches an address folded across a line end.
- **Accept (b):** "the header line's first label is `Address`, at x 1 (after its padding cell)".
- **Accept (c), and extend it to 118 columns.** With a vertical scrollbar the visible width at 118 is 58, not 60 (`[O deep]`). So a page with a 64-bit address gives *w* = 29, not 31. Correct `01-requirements.md:59` and `E1-rulings.md:42`: at 80, 12 cells (14 without a scrollbar); at 118 with a 64-bit address, 29 (31).
